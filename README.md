Hey — I'm **Azizbek**, from Samarkand, Uzbekistan.

I spend most of my time thinking about one question: **what if we moved compute infrastructure off Earth and into orbit?** Data centers already consume insane amounts of power and water just for cooling. Space gives you a 2.7K heat sink for free. The problem is, standard silicon doesn't survive out there — cosmic radiation destroys it.

So I'm researching **bio-inspired silicon architectures** that treat radiation hits as noise instead of crashes. I call the project **Hyperion**.

I'm also building real software, studying for MIT, and trying to figure things out as I go.

---

## what I'm actually working on

**Hyperion Silicon & Space Lab** — my main research project.  
The idea is simple: neuromorphic chip designs where cosmic ray strikes get absorbed as statistical noise in the network, not as fatal bit-flips. Paired with passive radiative cooling (Stefan-Boltzmann) instead of fans and water.

The governing equation for heat rejection in vacuum:

$$
Q = \varepsilon \sigma A (T^4 - T_{space}^4)
$$

No water. No fans. Just physics.
